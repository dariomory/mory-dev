Built internal process automation for [Kansaifixer](https://kansaifixer.com), a content and
lifestyle platform for immersive Japan travel, cultural education and local living experiences.

### The problem

A content operation looks like writing from the outside. From the inside it is mostly the work
around the writing: preparing material, moving it through to publication, keeping what already
exists accurate, and repeating all of that on every piece.

That surrounding work scales linearly with output while the writing does not, so past a certain
volume the routine becomes the thing limiting how much gets published. For a publication whose
value accumulates in its archive rather than in its newest post, that ceiling is expensive — it
caps the asset.

### Constraints

Content is the product, so anything introduced between having something to say and it being
live gets paid for in output. Automation had to remove steps rather than add a system to be
managed.

The people it serves are editorial, not technical. Tooling that requires an engineer to operate
does not survive contact with a publishing schedule, so it had to run without supervision and be
obvious when it did not.

### What I built

Automation for the internal processes behind the publication, in Python — the repetitive
operational work that happened the same way every time and only needed a person because nothing
else was doing it.

### Outcome

Less of the operation's time spent on routine and more of it available for the material itself,
which for a publication is the only input that matters.
