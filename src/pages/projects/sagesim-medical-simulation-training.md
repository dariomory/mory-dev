Core engineering on Sagesim, the medical simulation platform developed for Dr. Sage Wexner, MD,
providing immersive, hands-on training scenarios for medical students, residents, nurses and
healthcare professionals.

### The problem

Clinical judgement cannot be learned from reading. It comes from making decisions under
uncertainty and finding out what happened — which, in medicine, is exactly the thing you cannot
practise on real patients.

Simulation exists to close that gap. Its value is not in showing the right answer but in
letting a learner take a wrong path far enough to understand why it was wrong.

### Constraints

The content is authored by clinicians, not engineers. If building a scenario requires technical
skill, scenarios stop being written and the platform has nothing to teach with.

Learners also arrive at wildly different levels — a first-year student and a practising nurse
need different things from the same clinical situation. And a scenario that only rewards the
correct path teaches recognition rather than reasoning, so the branches that go wrong have to
be as carefully built as the ones that go right.

### What I built

Core engineering on the simulation platform — the scenario model and the mechanics that let a
learner work through a case, make decisions, and see the consequences play out.

### Outcome

A platform where clinical educators could put learners through decisions that matter, and where
being wrong was part of the training rather than the end of it.
