Core engineering on [Turf](https://useturf.io), the all-in-one community platform SaaS companies
use to boost user engagement, gather feedback and increase retention.

### The problem

Most SaaS communities die quietly. A forum gets launched, a handful of people post, and within
months it is a support queue with worse tooling. The difficulty is not building somewhere for
people to talk — it is that a community only becomes self-sustaining above a density of
activity that most products never reach.

That makes the software's job less about hosting discussion and more about concentrating it.

### Constraints

Attention is the scarce resource. Every notification, digest and prompt either brings someone
back or teaches them to ignore the product, and the line between those is narrow.

Feedback also has to be more than a suggestion box. Collected feedback is worthless unless it
can be aggregated into something a product team can act on, so structure has to be imposed
without making contribution feel like filing a ticket.

### What I built

Core engineering across the platform in **Node, React and GraphQL**.

GraphQL earns its place in a product like this: a community screen assembles posts, authors,
reactions, and permissions in one view, and the alternative is either a chain of round trips or
an endpoint per screen that ossifies as the product changes.

### Outcome

Community infrastructure aimed at the actual failure mode — engagement that compounds rather
than a forum that goes quiet — with feedback captured in a form a product team could use.
