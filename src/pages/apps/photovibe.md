Photovibe is a photo editor that runs entirely on your machine: tool column, options bar,
layers panel, blend modes, and the keyboard shortcuts you already know. If you have used
Photoshop, you can use it.

Working today: multi-layer documents with per-layer opacity and thirteen GPU-composited blend
modes; PNG, JPEG and WebP open and save; rectangular, freehand and magic-wand selections;
brush, eraser, gradient, healing and text tools; an eyedropper that samples anywhere on screen;
and full undo/redo with named history steps.

No subscription, no trial, no watermark, no upsell — and MIT licensed, so it stays free even
for commercial work. Installers are Authenticode-signed through Azure Trusted Signing.

This is early software. The editor is usable and under active development, and v0.1.0 is the
first public build.

### How it compares

Being straight about this: at v0.1.0 Photovibe is not competitive on features with any mature
editor. **GIMP** and **Krita** are free, cross-platform and have decades of work behind them.
**Photopea** runs in a browser and is remarkably close to the Photoshop interface. If you need
a capable editor today, use one of those.

What Photovibe is arguing for is a narrower thing: Photoshop muscle memory that works
immediately, a fully local GPU-composited engine with no account and no cloud, and an MIT
licence rather than a free tier. GIMP asks you to relearn its interface; Photopea needs a
browser and serves ads; Photoshop needs a subscription.
